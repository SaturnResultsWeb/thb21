# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Primarily couples in their 40s–50s planning a deliberate, unhurried break somewhere genuinely removed from the mainstream. They research carefully, value authenticity over prestige signalling, and make decisions together. Secondary audiences: high-income solo travelers who already know the Channel Islands and are comparing premium options; groups or families booking a private house hire. All users are arriving with intent — they are not browsing; they are deciding.

## Product Purpose

Convert consideration into direct bookings and enquiries for The Blonde Hedgehog, a Michelin Key boutique hotel in Alderney, Channel Islands. The Michelin Key award (one of only 2 in the Channel Islands) is the primary trust signal. Success on the homepage is a booking click-through or a WhatsApp/email enquiry initiated. The site must do the work of convincing a couple in their 40s that this remote island hotel is worth the journey — and then make booking feel effortless.

## Brand Personality

Luxury, Unhurried, Friendly. Warm without being fussy. The hotel is genuinely special — Michelin Key, farm-to-table, four restored historic buildings — but it wears this lightly. No five-star pomp, no cold opulence. The tone is assured and personal, not corporate. Guests should feel welcomed, not impressed.

## Anti-references

- **Generic hotel chains** (Marriott, Hilton, IHG): corporate templates, identical layouts, stock photography vibes, award badges used as wallpaper.
- **Over-designed boutique hotel sites**: black-heavy, serif-maximalist, editorial overload, trying too hard. Design that announces itself before the place does.
- **Quaint British B&B aesthetic**: cottage-cosy, floral, small-scale, dated. The Blonde Hedgehog is a Michelin Key property, not a guesthouse.
- **Boring**: flat layouts, predictable section order, nothing that makes you stop scrolling.

## Design Principles

1. **Warmth before grandeur.** The Michelin Key matters, but it should feel earned through hospitality, not announced through opulence. Never let prestige crowd out approachability.

2. **The island is half the product.** Alderney's remoteness, slow pace, and natural beauty are as much the offer as the rooms. Design should breathe. Space is not emptiness — it is the point.

3. **Confidence without performance.** A couple in their 40s researching this trip doesn't need convincing the hotel is good. They need to feel it. Show the reality; don't announce the credentials.

4. **Booking is the destination.** Every section compounds toward a reservation or enquiry. Interest should not dissipate into content. Each scroll step should make the next step clearer.

5. **The anti-generic mandate.** Generic is the primary enemy. Every layout, component, and copy decision must pass the test: could this be any hotel? If yes, rework it.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA as standard. No specific user needs identified beyond that. Ensure adequate contrast on all text over imagery (hero sections, card overlays). Support reduced motion via `prefers-reduced-motion`. No colour-only information encoding.
